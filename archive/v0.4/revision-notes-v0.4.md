# Revision notes: v0.3 → v0.4

## Purpose

This revision incorporates the targeted literature review requested after v0.3. It deliberately narrows claims of novelty while expanding the paper's research direction beyond a purely pedagogical FLRW visualization.

## Main changes

- Reframed the abstract and introduction to state that null propagation, hyperspherical embeddings, polar projections, and geodesic spiralling all have precedents.
- Added a related-work subsection distinguishing the present mapping from conformal/proper-distance diagrams and from Sherard's non-peer-reviewed S-1 visualization.
- Added a Bianchi/anisotropy subsection. Exact FLRW is identified as a zero-shear, zero-vorticity baseline; Bianchi VII_h geodesic spiralling is treated as established prior literature rather than as a prediction of this model.
- Added the covariant vorticity tensor and made clear that raw polar winding is coordinate- and plotting-scale-dependent.
- Added a local-vorticity subsection covering simulations and observations of rotating cosmic filaments. The Tudorache et al. ~110 km/s result is explicitly described as filament rotation, not universal rotation.
- Added a four-stage research program: FLRW → Bianchi I → Bianchi VII_h → localized vortical perturbations.
- Updated the conclusion: the strongest prospective contribution is a comparative geometrical method, not a new cosmological theory.
- Added references [13]–[18].

## Claims intentionally NOT made

- The 110 km/s filament result is not evidence that the Universe rotates globally.
- The temporal-radial winding itself is not an observable vorticity signal.
- No coordinate-invariant residual-winding diagnostic has yet been derived.
- No claim of priority for polar cosmological diagrams is made.
- No new fit to CMB, galaxy-spin, filament, or expansion data is presented.

## Suggested next mathematical task

Derive a tetrad-based null-propagation comparison between FLRW and a simple Bianchi model. A useful v0.5 should only claim a new methodological result if that derivation yields an invariant or directly observable quantity not reducible to plotting convention.
